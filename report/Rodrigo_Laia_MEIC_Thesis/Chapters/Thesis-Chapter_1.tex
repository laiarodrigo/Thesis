\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:intro-motivation}

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used for multilingual text processing,
including tasks such as classification, generation, translation, and structured
text transformation \citep{zhao2025surveylargelanguagemodels}. Even so, this
success does not change the basic limitation that their behavior remains shaped
by the distribution of the data on which they were trained. For languages with
multiple standardized varieties, this creates a practical problem.
Comparable challenges arise in languages such as English, Spanish, Arabic, or
French, where users may expect computational systems to respect the variety
they use rather than the one most represented in the training data
\citep{zampieri2020natural}.

Brazilian Portuguese is disproportionately represented in Portuguese text
available on the web, and resources labeled simply as Portuguese tend to
overrepresent Brazilian usage. That imbalance can propagate to pretrained
models, which may treat Brazilian Portuguese as the unmarked default and
under-serve European Portuguese users
\citep{sousa2025enhancingportuguesevarietyidentification}.

Portuguese is a pluricentric language shared across countries and speech
communities whose varieties have been shaped by geographic
separation, migration, institutional standardization, media ecosystems, and
local cultural practices. Among these varieties, European Portuguese (EP) and
Brazilian Portuguese (BP) are studied here as two national standards with
differences in spelling, lexical choice, discourse conventions, and
morphosyntactic preferences such as clitic placement, verbal periphrases,
subject realization, or forms of address
\citep{barreiro-mota-2018-paraphrastic}. Therefore, rewriting between EP and BP
often has to remain conservative, with many sentences requiring only localized
lexical or morphosyntactic changes and others remaining unchanged.

Computationally, these differences are systematic enough to affect model
outputs, while remaining easy to ignore during data collection, annotation,
and evaluation. As a result, corpora labeled only at the language level often
mix multiple varieties, and models trained on them can blur distinctions that
remain important to users. Automatic filtering can further affect this
representation when corpus-construction pipelines depend on
language-identification predictions. Under domain mismatch, class imbalance,
and language similarity, identification errors can propagate into the
composition of web corpora \citep{caswell-etal-2020-language-id-wild}.

\section{Research Statement}
\label{sec:intro-research-statement}

Research on Natural Language Processing (NLP) for language varieties has grown
in recent years, but work that simultaneously studies EP/BP identification, EP/BP
rewriting, and adaptation under curated supervision remains
comparatively scarce. Portuguese is used in this work as a case study for that broader
problem. Rather than treating Portuguese as a single homogeneous language, this
dissertation studies how corpus construction, task formulation, supervision
composition, control placement, and adaptation strategy influence
variety-aware behavior.

Accordingly, the central objective of this dissertation is to determine how
pretrained encoder-decoder models can be adapted to identify and rewrite EP/BP
varieties under data constraints. More specifically, it aims to determine how
preprocessing, filtering, and corpus composition affect the supervision
available for training and evaluation, and to investigate how task and variety
information should be represented within a shared encoder-decoder interface.
In the rewriting task, textual control strings indicate the requested
operation and the variety of the input sentence, which determines the
rewriting direction. The study compares placing control information on the
encoder side with the variety label on the decoder side.

The experimental study uses T5Gemma 2 models with 270M and 4B parameters for
different experimental roles. The 270M-parameter model is fully fine-tuned for lower-cost supervised protocol
selection and separate reward-design diagnostics, whereas the 4B-parameter
model is adapted with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) \citep{hu2022lora} for the
control, data-composition, and staged-training
experiments. To investigate these questions, the experiments with the 4B-parameter model
follow a three-stage adaptation pipeline. Stage A provides broad supervised EP/BP exposure through
OpenSubtitles, Stage B performs targeted specialization with GPT-Wikipedia
either alone or with additional Few-shot Region-aware Machine Translation (FRMT) supervision, and Stage C applies
reward-based continuation from the corresponding Stage B checkpoints using
more conservative rewriting-only subsets. The resulting models are evaluated
on the Golden Collection
\citep{sanches2024brazilianportugueseeuropeanportuguese} and FRMT
\citep{riley2023frmtbenchmarkfewshotregionaware}, providing
evaluation settings for analyzing variety identification and rewriting behavior.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contributions}

Accordingly, this research project makes four main contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item It consolidates reusable EP-BP data resources from multiple sources.
    Sources include OpenSubtitles \citep{4992de1b5fb34f3e9691772606b36edf},
    the Golden Collection
    \citep{sanches2024brazilianportugueseeuropeanportuguese}, FRMT
    \citep{riley2023frmtbenchmarkfewshotregionaware}, and an additional
    GPT-Wikipedia resource. The associated preprocessing and filtering\hfil\break
    procedures are adapted to the characteristics of each source.
    \item It develops and evaluates models for both\hfil\break
    language variety
    identification and bidirectional rewriting between EP and BP.
    \item It studies encoder-decoder adaptation through lower-cost full-fine-tuning
experiments with the 270M-parameter T5Gemma 2 model for supervised protocol
selection and LoRA experiments with the 4B-parameter model covering control
formulation, data composition, staged training, and reward-based continuation.
    \item It complements aggregate evaluation with corpus and qualitative error
    analyses, highlighting data strengths, model failures, and linguistic
    phenomena that remain difficult after fine-tuning.
\end{enumerate}

These contributions are connected through the data and modeling choices made in
the dissertation. On the data side, corpus construction makes heterogeneous
resources comparable. OpenSubtitles, the Golden Collection, FRMT, and
GPT-Wikipedia differ in origin, format, noise profile, alignment quality, and
supervision type. For that reason, this work includes preprocessing, filtering,
and task-rendering steps that convert these materials into shared formats for
EP/BP identification and rewriting, rather than treating them as directly
interchangeable inputs.

On the modeling side, the contribution is a study of T5Gemma 2
\citep{zhang2025t5gemma2seeingreading} encoder-decoder models with 270 million
and 4 billion parameters. The 270M-parameter model is fully fine-tuned to compare alternative
supervised-training protocols before the experiments with the 4B-parameter
model.
Evaluation uses accuracy and macro-F1
for identification, and Bilingual Evaluation Understudy (BLEU)
\citep{papineni2002bleu} and Translation Edit Rate (TER)
\citep{snover2006study} for rewriting. In addition to these quantitative
metrics, the analysis examines copy behavior, conservative edits,
paraphrasing, and over-generation, which are important in this setting where
the preferred output should remain close to the source and change only what is
necessary. This makes the evaluation sensitive to data-quality problems that
may be hidden by corpus-level scores alone.

The experiments built from these contributions point to a conditional pattern.
Among the supervised comparisons, data composition produces the largest
rewriting shifts. GPT-Wikipedia aligns most closely with conservative Golden
Collection rewriting, whereas the GPT-Wikipedia + FRMT data mix encourages broader changes and
lowers Golden Collection performance. The effects of control placement and
staged adaptation vary across resources and rewriting directions. Reward-based
continuation changes the GPT-Wikipedia conditions modestly while partly
recovering Golden Collection performance in the GPT-Wikipedia + FRMT conditions.
The copy analysis shows that overlap metrics reward restraint but cannot
distinguish necessary
variety adaptation, acceptable paraphrase, and unnecessary reformulation.

\section{Document Organization}
\label{sec:intro-document-organization}

The remainder of this dissertation is organized as follows.
\Cref{chap:back} introduces the linguistic and technical background required
to understand the problem and the modeling choices considered in this work.
\Cref{chap:related_work} reviews previous work on Portuguese variety
identification and rewriting, broader language-variety processing, sequential
adaptation, and reward-based fine-tuning. \Cref{chap:data} details the
corpora, preprocessing procedures, filtering decisions, and stage-level data
allocation. \Cref{chap:methodology} describes the models, task formulations,
training stages, adaptation strategies, and reward-based continuation setup.
\Cref{chap:evaluation} presents the experimental methodology and analyzes the
identification and rewriting results. Finally, \Cref{chap:conclusion}
summarizes the main findings and outlines future research.
