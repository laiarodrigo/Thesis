\noindent Portuguese is a pluricentric language, but large language models trained
on generic web corpora are often shaped by the predominance of Brazilian
Portuguese. This dissertation studies language-variety processing for European Portuguese (EP) and Brazilian Portuguese (BP) through two related tasks: sentence-level variety
identification and bidirectional rewriting. The work consolidates supervision
from OpenSubtitles, Few-shot Region-aware Machine Translation (FRMT), a manually curated Golden Collection, and a
GPT-Wikipedia resource, applying preprocessing and filtering procedures aimed
at preserving variety-relevant differences while reducing noisy paraphrase and
alignment errors.

The experimental study adapts T5Gemma 2 encoder-decoder models with 270M and 4B
parameters. The 270M-parameter T5Gemma 2 model is fully fine-tuned to test
decoder-controlled protocol choices before the subsequent experiments, while
the 4B-parameter model is adapted with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). The study also compares encoder-side control strings with decoder-side variety labels and evaluates a three-stage training pipeline composed of broad supervised adaptation, targeted supervised specialization, and reward-based continuation. Additional diagnostics analyze LoRA rank and isolated
reward-design variants. Among the matched supervised comparisons, data
composition produces the largest observed changes in rewriting performance.
Supervision aligned with localized rewriting is strongest on the conservative
Golden Collection benchmark, while adding supervision from FRMT strongly
lowers Golden Collection rewriting and produces formulation- and
direction-dependent FRMT effects. Control placement is therefore conditional
rather than universally ordered. Reward-based continuation partly restores
Golden Collection performance for the GPT-Wikipedia + FRMT conditions. These findings make
supervision quality and evaluation central to Portuguese variety adaptation:
aligned EP/BP resources can encourage different rewriting behavior, while
automatic metrics remain highly sensitive to copying and do not fully
distinguish necessary adaptation from broader reformulation.
