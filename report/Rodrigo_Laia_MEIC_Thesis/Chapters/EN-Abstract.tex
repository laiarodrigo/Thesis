\noindent Portuguese has multiple varieties, but language models are shaped by the predominance of Brazilian Portuguese in the data used for training. This work studies European Portuguese (EP) and Brazilian Portuguese (BP) through two tasks: variety identification and bidirectional rewriting.

The models use data from OpenSubtitles, Few-shot Region-aware Machine Translation (FRMT), the Golden Collection, and GPT-Wikipedia, a resource created from pairs focused only on differences between the varieties and processed to reduce unnecessary changes.

We use 270M- and 4B-parameter T5Gemma 2 encoder-decoder models. The 270M model is fully fine-tuned to select the training protocol, while the 4B model uses Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to compare control placement, data composition, and three-stage training.

Data composition produces the largest differences in rewriting quality. Models trained only with GPT-Wikipedia achieve better results on the Golden Collection, where sentences require few changes. Adding FRMT improves identification on that dataset and reduces performance on the Golden Collection, with rewriting effects depending on direction and control placement. Decoder control performs better in every BP-to-EP comparison, while EP-to-BP results are mixed. Reinforcement Learning continuation changes the GPT-Wikipedia models only slightly but substantially recovers Golden Collection performance after GPT-Wikipedia + FRMT. The results also show that automatic metrics do not always distinguish necessary changes from appropriate preservation or broader reformulations.
